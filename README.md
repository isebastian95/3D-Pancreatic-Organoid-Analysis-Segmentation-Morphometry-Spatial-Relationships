# 3D-Pancreatic-Organoid-Analysis-Segmentation-Morphometry-Spatial-Relationships

## Scientific Background and Contributions

This project focuses on the development and optimization of customized 3D image-analysis pipelines for pancreatic organoid and assembloid microscopy datasets.

The broader research investigates pancreatic organoid organization, cellular morphology, and dynamic biological processes using fluorescence microscopy, including 3D time-lapse imaging.

### My Contributions

As part of a collaborative research project, I developed and optimized computational image-analysis workflows using **ZEISS arivis** and the **Apeer deep learning platform**.

My contributions include:

- **3D nuclear segmentation:** Identification and quantification of nuclei using fluorescence-based preprocessing, classical segmentation methods, and custom deep learning approaches.
- **Membrane-based cell segmentation:** Segmentation and morphological characterization of individual cells using optimized filtering, intensity thresholds, and splitting parameters.
- **Organoid morphology analysis:** Segmentation and quantitative characterization of pancreatic organoids using membrane fluorescence, morphological operations, and size-based filtering.
- **Spatial relationship analysis:** Development and application of object-based analysis workflows to identify nuclei contained within individual cells and cells contained within organoids. Spatial relationships were evaluated using containment and intersection criteria.
- **Quantitative feature extraction:** Measurement of nuclear and cellular counts, object volumes, sphericity, and compartment-specific object counts.
- **Segmentation quality control:** Optimization of segmentation parameters, visual validation of detected objects, and manual correction of incomplete segmentation where necessary.

### Scope

This repository documents my contributions to the computational analysis of pancreatic organoid and assembloid microscopy datasets.

F-actin structural analysis was also performed within the broader research project but is not included among my individual contributions.
